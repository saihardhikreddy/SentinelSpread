Chapter 01 — Command module

Sealed at the source.

Behind the bay door of the command module, a fresh AES-256-GCM key locks the message and RSA-OAEP wraps that key for the receiver at home. What leaves the core cannot be told apart from random bits.

Chapter 02 — High-gain antenna

Sixteen chips per bit.

The downlink multiplies every bit by a pseudo-noise code running sixteen times faster, smearing its energy below the noise floor. On the ground, a Costas loop cancels the drift and the same code collapses it back into bits.

Chapter 03 — Imaging module

A picture that carries more.

The payload is written into the least significant bit of each pixel, a change of one level in 255 that the eye cannot see. A watermark can be stamped on top, visibly or hidden in the image's DCT coefficients.

Chapter 04 — Comms module

A voice that carries more.

For sound, the message is encrypted with AES-128-CBC under your password, tagged with an ASTG header, and written into the lowest bit of 16-bit PCM samples. The recording sounds the same; only the password brings the text back.

Every tool, on the ground.

Everything SENTINEL does runs in the workbench below, against your local SentinelSpread API.

Workbench

Twenty tools, one at a time. Everything runs against your local SentinelSpread API, and files never leave this machine.

Transmit a message

Encrypt, spread and send a message through the GNU Radio software radio, listen to the signal, then despread and decrypt it again.

POST /api/radio/transmit · receive

You review the numbers first. Nothing is sent until you press Transmit.

Hide text in an image

Hide text in audio
